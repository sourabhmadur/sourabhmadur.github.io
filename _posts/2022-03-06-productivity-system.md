Over the years, I've used google keep, apple notes, notion, and a few to-do list apps to stay on top of things and get stuff done. Among all these, I've found a combination of notion and old-school pen-paper to-do lists to be the most effective.


### Creating Digital Plan Docs

Being productive requires planning. Unfortunately, planning for any goal is not trivial and needs a lot of time and effort. This is by and large a consequence of planning being an iterative process where the [DAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_acyclic_graph) of tasks required to achieve the goal is evolving as we work towards it. Also, each goal is different from the other, and trying to force any common planning structure on all would be counterproductive. For example, the type of tasks that are needed to be done to learn a programming language might be completely different from the tasks required to lose weight. 

To deal with these challenges, I usually create a one-page document for each of my goals. This page has a loose structure and is a living and breathing document that evolves constantly and helps me focus on working towards the goal. This is inspired by the [V2MOM technique](https://tinyurl.com/y78t7fkz) evangelized by Marc Benioff

The most important part of this document is having metrics for measuring progress and documenting learnings for constant progress.

To make it concrete, let's see an example where your long-term goal is to lose 10 Kg in 5 months. 

The first task would be to research methods for how to lose weight. The document could initially look like this:

```
Iteration 1:

   Vision : Loose 10kg over 5 mo
      
   Method DAG
      Research techniques on how to loose weight 
         - read blogs
         - watch yt videos
         - read magazines

   Obstacles:

   Metrics
      Current Weight : 65Kg
      
```

After this first iteration, I work on the tasks, which in this case, is to do research. After this is done, the modified doc could look something like this:

```
Iteration 2: 

   Vision: Loose 10kg over 5 mo

   Method DAG
     - Eat good food 
      - meal plan weekly
         - cut down on sugar
         - macro and micro composition
         - create a list of dishes to try

     - Stay hydrated
      - drink 5L water per day

     - Have enough sleep
      - commit to 8hr sleep every day

     - Exercise
      -  weekly HIIT plan

  - Metrics
   - Measure bi-weekly weight losses and iterate on the plan
   Current Weight 64.5kg

  - Obstacles:

 - Learnings/ Modifications to the plan:
   [] 
```

If after a few months of following this plan, let us say it is not giving any results(look at the metrics section). You identify the cause to be the number of carbs in your diet. You, take that into account while weekly prepping and add this learning to the learnings field and modify the plan:

```
Iteration 3: 

   Loose weight
     - Eat good food 
      - meal plan weekly
         - cut down on sugar
         - macro and micro composition
         - create a list of dishes to try
         - Eat low carbs 

     - Stay hydrated
      - drink 5L water per day

     - Have enough sleep
      - commit to 8hr sleep every day

     - Exercise
      -  weekly HIIT plan

     - Metrics
      - Measure bi-weekly weight losses and iterate on the plan


    - Learnings/ Modifications to the plan:
      [eat low carbs] 

```

This process can be repeated until the metrics reflect the completion of the goal.


### Physical To-Do Lists

For my day-to-day planning, I create a to-do list by taking into account the current projects, deadlines, and priorities from the Notion docs. This daily planning process forces me to think about the big picture and not get too lost in the details while working on the daily tasks. The notion docs serve as a Northstar in this process. Once the to-do lists are created for the day, I focus on taking them down one by one.


In addition to its simplicity, the to-do list has multiple advantages. Firstly, the to-do list naturally fits into the [timeboxing framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeboxing) and this helps me to not context switch a lot. Secondly, to-do lists are free to form. As I use pen and paper, there is no defined structure to the tasks. This provides the flexibility to connect the tasks, create diagrams, create checkboxes, and write down anything that I think of. Finally, striking off tasks on the lists is very satisfying and gives a sense of accomplishment, which is important to build momentum.
